This guide explains the main Florida injunction types, what to gather before filing, and what to expect afterward. ## What is a restraining order called in Florida?

People commonly use "restraining order" and "protective order" in everyday conversation. Florida courts generally use the term *injunction for protection*. An injunction is a court order that can prohibit certain conduct or contact and may address other safety-related issues authorized by law.

The [Duval County Clerk explains](https://www.duvalclerk.com/departments/civil-court-services/domestic-violence) that an injunction may restrict contact and, depending on the circumstances, address matters such as a temporary parenting plan, temporary child support, counseling, treatment, or other conditions. The specific relief is decided by a judge. Filing a petition does not guarantee that every request will be granted.

Florida has separate petition forms for different kinds of violence and relationships. Choosing the form that fits the facts is one of the most important steps before filing.

## Which type of Florida injunction may apply?

Florida protective injunctions are not one-size-fits-all. The categories below are a starting point, not a legal determination. Read the official form instructions and consider speaking with a lawyer if you are unsure which petition describes your situation.

| Injunction type   | May fit when                                                                                                     | Important point                                   |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Domestic violence | The respondent is a qualifying family or household member, spouse, former spouse, or other parent of your child. | Relationship and danger facts both matter.        |
| Repeat violence   | There have been at least two acts of violence, including one within the prior six months.                        | Describe each incident and its date.              |
| Dating violence   | The parties had a qualifying romantic or intimate relationship.                                                  | A casual social relationship may not qualify.     |
| Sexual violence   | The petition concerns sexual violence and the statutory requirements are met.                                    | Reporting and cooperation requirements can apply. |
| Stalking          | The respondent has repeatedly followed, harassed, or cyberstalked the petitioner.                                | Preserve electronic evidence and dates.           |

### Domestic violence injunction

A domestic violence petition may apply when the respondent is a spouse or former spouse, a family or household member who lives with you or previously lived with you as a family, or the other parent of your child. Florida law also covers situations involving acts of domestic violence or an imminent danger of domestic violence. The relationship details matter, so do not assume that any difficult relationship belongs in this category.

### Repeat violence injunction

A repeat violence petition generally concerns at least two acts of violence by the same person, with one act occurring within the six months before filing. The acts and their dates should be described specifically. The violence does not have to involve a current or former intimate relationship for this category to be relevant.

### Dating violence injunction

Dating violence involves a romantic or intimate relationship that existed within the period described by Florida law. A casual acquaintance or ordinary social relationship may not meet the legal definition. The nature and continuity of the relationship, along with the alleged acts, are part of the court's analysis.

### Sexual violence injunction

Florida Courts instructions state that, to obtain this type of injunction, the petitioner generally must have reported the sexual violence to law enforcement and be cooperating in any criminal proceeding, if one exists. Criminal charges may have been filed, reduced, or dismissed. A separate route may apply when the respondent was imprisoned and is out of prison or expected to be released within 90 days. Review the current [Form 12.980(q) instructions](https://flcourts-media.flcourts.gov/content/download/685970/file_pdf/980q.pdf) because the statutory definition and filing requirements are specific.

### Stalking or cyberstalking injunction

A stalking injunction may apply to repeated following, harassment, or cyberstalking. Florida law treats cyberstalking as part of the stalking injunction cause of action. Electronic messages, online conduct, and other communications may matter when they form the required course of conduct. Save the original messages and record dates rather than relying only on memory. The [Florida stalking-injunction statute](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute\&Search_String=\&URL=0700-0799%2F0784%2FSections%2F0784.0485.html) provides the current legal framework.

The [Florida Courts family law forms page](https://www.flcourts.gov/Services/Family-Courts/domestic-relations-court-resources/family-law-forms/domestic-violence-12.980-forms-a-e) provides the official petition forms and instructions. ## What should you gather before filing?

A petition asks you to explain what happened and why protection is needed. Clear, concrete facts are more useful than labels or general conclusions. Before filing, consider organizing the following:

* **A chronological account.** Write down dates, approximate times, locations, what was said or done, and whether anyone saw or heard it. Include the most recent incident and earlier incidents that show a pattern.
* **Relationship information.** Be ready to explain how you know the respondent and whether you share a home, child, or other family connection.
* **Contact and service information.** You generally need the respondent's full name and an address where the sheriff or another authorized law enforcement officer may be able to serve the papers. If you do not know an address, ask the clerk what information is required and what options exist.
* **Supporting records.** Preserve photographs, medical records, police reports, call logs, messages, emails, social media posts, security video, and names of witnesses when available. Do not edit screenshots in a way that removes context. Keep copies in a secure location.
* **Information about children, housing, and pets.** If the situation affects a shared home, a child, or an animal, gather the existing court orders and basic identifying information. The court may have authority to address some temporary issues, but an injunction is not a replacement for every family law case.

Use care when preparing the petition. Florida's domestic violence form includes a sworn statement that the facts are true under penalties of perjury. Do not exaggerate, guess at facts, or include information that you cannot support. If you are using a device that another person may monitor, the Duval Clerk recommends considering a safer device and reviewing its online safety guidance.

## Where do you file a Florida restraining order?

Protective injunctions are filed with the clerk of the circuit court. The proper location can depend on the type of injunction and the facts. Florida statutes commonly allow filing where the petitioner lives or temporarily resides, where the respondent lives, or where the violence or stalking occurred. There is not always a minimum residency requirement, but venue rules should be confirmed for the petition you plan to file.

If you are filing in Jacksonville or elsewhere in Duval County, the [Duval County Clerk's domestic violence page](https://www.duvalclerk.com/departments/civil-court-services/domestic-violence) currently lists electronic filing, mail, and in-person filing through its Domestic Violence Department at 501 West Adams Street, Room 2409, Jacksonville, Florida 32202. It lists in-person hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Check the clerk's current instructions before traveling because locations, hours, and filing procedures can change.

The clerk's office can provide procedural assistance and forms, but clerks generally cannot give legal advice or tell you which strategy is best. That distinction matters if you are deciding what facts to include, which category applies, or how an injunction may interact with an existing divorce, custody, or criminal matter.

## What happens after you file?

After filing, a judge reviews the petition. Depending on the facts, the judge may issue a temporary injunction without first hearing from the respondent, deny temporary relief and set a hearing, or take another action allowed by law. An ex parte temporary injunction means the judge initially considers the information presented by the petitioner. It is temporary and is not the final decision on the case.

Under the Florida Courts instructions for the domestic violence petition, a temporary injunction generally lasts until the full hearing or for up to 15 days, whichever comes first, although a court may extend it for a legally permitted reason. The court will set a hearing when required, and the respondent will have an opportunity to respond. The exact schedule depends on the court and the case.

Service is a critical step. A temporary injunction generally must be served on the respondent before it becomes effective against that person. Until you confirm service and understand the order's effective date, do not assume that an order has been enforced. If you are the protected person, continue using a safety plan and contact law enforcement if you are in immediate danger.

## What should you expect at the injunction hearing?

At a final hearing, both sides may have an opportunity to present testimony and evidence. Bring the information that supports the facts in your petition, follow the court's instructions, and answer questions directly. Organize your evidence in date order and identify the facts you personally observed. Witnesses, photographs, messages, police records, and other admissible evidence may be relevant, but the judge decides what the court will consider.

Do not treat a hearing as a chance to argue every disagreement in a relationship. Focus on the conduct and facts that relate to the legal standard for the petition. If children, a pending divorce, or another court order is involved, tell your attorney and bring copies of relevant orders. Missing a hearing can affect the temporary injunction and the court's next steps, so contact the clerk or your attorney promptly if an emergency prevents attendance.

## What can an injunction require?

The wording of an injunction controls. Depending on the category and evidence, an order may prohibit violence, stalking, or contact; require the respondent to stay away from specified people or places; address a shared residence; or provide temporary parenting or time-sharing terms. Florida law also permits other forms of relief in some circumstances.

Read every page of an order, including any attachments. If you are the respondent, do not assume that a message through a friend, a social media reaction, a tag, or another indirect contact is allowed. The firm's earlier article on [domestic violence orders and social media](https://www.jimmullaney.com/blog/2016/01/domestic-violence-orders-and-social-media/) discusses why electronic activity can create problems when an order prohibits contact. If a term is unclear, ask a lawyer or the court for guidance rather than testing the boundary.

## How does a protective injunction relate to divorce or custody?

A protective injunction and a divorce or custody case are related only in limited ways. An injunction may include temporary provisions involving children or the shared home, but it does not automatically decide every issue in a divorce. Property division, alimony, a permanent parenting plan, child support, and other family law matters may require separate pleadings, evidence, and orders.

If both spouses want a safe and workable agreement on long-term family law issues, mediation may be appropriate only when participation is voluntary and safe. Mediation is not a substitute for emergency protection, and it is not suitable for every situation. ## Key points to remember

* Florida usually calls a restraining order an injunction for protection.
* The correct petition depends on the relationship, conduct, and facts.
* Specific dates, descriptions, records, and service information can help you prepare.

## Frequently asked questions about Florida restraining orders

### Is a restraining order the same as an injunction in Florida?

In everyday conversation, people often use the terms interchangeably. Florida courts generally use *injunction for protection* for these protective orders. The petition type and requested relief depend on the relationship and conduct involved.

### How long does a temporary injunction last?

A temporary domestic violence injunction generally lasts until the full hearing or for up to 15 days, whichever comes first. The court may extend it when the law permits, so read the order and hearing notice carefully rather than relying on a general timeline.

### Do I have to pay to file an injunction in Duval County?

The Duval County Clerk states that there is no fee to file an injunction for protection. Local procedures can change, so confirm current instructions with the clerk before filing.

### Can an injunction affect parenting time?

In some circumstances, a judge may include temporary parenting or time-sharing provisions in an injunction. That temporary relief does not automatically resolve the entire parenting-plan or custody case. Bring existing court orders to your attorney's attention.

### What if the respondent contacts me after an order is entered?

Follow the order's instructions and preserve evidence of the contact. If you believe there is an immediate safety risk or a violation, contact law enforcement. Do not respond in a way that could create a dispute about whether you also violated a no-contact term. A lawyer can help you understand the next step.
