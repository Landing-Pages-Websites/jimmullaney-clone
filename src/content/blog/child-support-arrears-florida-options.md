If you are looking for child support arrears Florida options, start by identifying whether you owe overdue support or are trying to collect it. Arrears do not disappear simply because a parent has lost a job, the child is older, or the parents have made an informal arrangement. The right next step depends on the court order, payment history, and whether the balance is accurate.

## What are child support arrears?

Arrears are unpaid child support that accrued under an order. The court order remains the starting point: parents generally should not change the ordered amount on their own, even if circumstances change. An informal agreement to accept less or pause payments may not change the legal obligation or the recorded balance.

First, obtain the current order and a payment history. Review the amount due each month, payments credited, and any fees or adjustments shown by the agency or court. Keep receipts for direct payments, but do not assume an unrecorded payment has been credited. Ask how to submit proof so the official record can be corrected.

## What options may help resolve unpaid support?

### Use Florida child support enforcement services

A parent seeking collection can ask the Florida Child Support Program about enforcement and account information. Depending on the case and applicable law, enforcement may include income withholding, interception of certain payments, or other authorized collection measures. For an overview of available services and procedures, consult the [Florida Department of Revenue Child Support Program](https://floridarevenue.com/childsupport/Pages/default.aspx). Enforcement steps depend on the facts and should not be treated as guaranteed.

When support is paid through a state agency, use its instructions to report missed payments, update contact or employment details, and provide records. If support is paid directly under the order, carefully document each payment and communicate through a method that creates a reliable record.

### Discuss a realistic payment arrangement

If you owe arrears and cannot pay the full balance at once, a proposed payment schedule may be worth discussing with the other parent or the agency handling the case. Be specific about what you can pay toward current support and what you can pay toward the overdue amount. A proposal should reflect income, necessary expenses, and the existing order.

A private arrangement does not automatically alter a court order or prevent enforcement. Before relying on any agreement, find out whether it needs to be submitted to the court or handled through the child support agency. Keep making payments required by the order unless a court changes it.

### Ask the court to review support when circumstances change

If income or another relevant circumstance has materially changed, a parent may consider seeking a modification of ongoing support. A modification generally concerns future support; it does not automatically erase arrears that already accrued. The court must approve a change, and the effective date and outcome depend on the case. Do not wait while unpaid amounts continue to accumulate.

## What if the arrears balance is wrong?

Disputes can arise when payments were made directly, an employer withheld support but the payment has not appeared, or the record contains an incorrect amount. Gather bank statements, money order receipts, wage statements, agency notices, and the order. Make a simple month-by-month list showing the amount due, what was paid, when, and how.

* Request a payment accounting from the agency or entity maintaining the record.
* Identify each payment you believe is missing or misapplied.
* Provide copies of supporting documents and keep the originals.
* Ask what process is available to challenge or correct the accounting.

A parent should not treat a disputed balance as cancelled while waiting for review. Continue complying with current obligations and respond promptly to court or agency notices. If a hearing is scheduled, organize the timeline and bring copies of the relevant records.

## What should you do first?

Whether you are owed support or have fallen behind, these steps can help clarify the situation:

1. Read the latest court order and note the amount, due date, and payment method.
2. Obtain the official account history and compare it with your records.
3. Separate current monthly support from past-due amounts.
4. Document changes in income or other circumstances that may matter to a request for modification.
5. Use the appropriate agency or court process rather than relying only on verbal promises.

## When should you get legal guidance?

Consider speaking with a family law attorney if the balance is substantial or contested, enforcement action has begun, you have received a court notice, or you need to request a change to ongoing support. Legal guidance can help distinguish a dispute over accounting from a request to modify the order and identify the proper procedure. In some cases, formal court action is necessary; resolution through discussion is not appropriate or possible in every situation.
